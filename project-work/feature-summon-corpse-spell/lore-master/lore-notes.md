# Universal Summon Corpse Spell — Lore Notes

> **Feature branch:** `feature/summon-corpse-spell`
> **Author:** lore-master
> **Date started:** 2026-05-03

---

## Feature Concept

A universal summon-corpse spell granted to all 12 casting-capable classes (CLR, DRU, SHM, NEC, WIZ, MAG, ENC, PAL, SHD, RNG, BST, BRD). Free, level 1, zero mana, 6-second cast, 3-minute cooldown. Pulls the caster's own corpse to their feet within the same zone. The core lore tension: corpse summoning is firmly Necromancer and Shadow Knight territory in Classic-Luclin EQ — the live spells even require coffin reagents, marking them as funerary/death magic. Extending the ability universally to holy classes (Paladin, Cleric), nature classes (Druid, Ranger), and arcane specialists (Wizard, Magician, Enchanter, Bard) requires a coherent in-world rationale.

## Lore Research

### Class and Deity Backgrounds Relevant to This Feature

**Necromancer / Shadow Knight (natural fit)**
Necromancers and Shadow Knights are the only classes with native corpse-summon spells in live Classic EQ. NEC gets Lesser Summon Corpse at level 12 and Summon Corpse at level 39; SHD gets Lesser Summon Corpse at level 25 and Summon Corpse at level 51. Both spells require coffin-shaped reagents (Black Ceremonial Coffin, Jade Inlaid Coffin), anchoring them in funerary/death-magic tradition. Shadow Knights worship Innoruuk, Cazic-Thule, or Bertoxxulous — all death/fear/hate-adjacent deities. The possessive construction in SK spell names (e.g., "Innoruuk's Caress") is established canon.

**Cleric (divine authority over death)**
Clerics hold exclusive dominion over Resurrection magic in Classic EQ — they alone can return the soul with experience regained. Their power flows from deity authority, not personal holiness. "Hallowed" (blessed ground/object) reads as Paladin-flavored; Cleric magic is more invocatory (the deity acts through the cleric). Clerics worship diverse gods including Tunare, Innoruuk (dark elf clerics), Rodcet Nife, Erollisi Marr, Mithaniel Marr, Brell Serilis. Evil-deity clerics exist and are mechanically viable.

**Paladin (holy warrior, divine agent)**
Paladins are holy warriors of good-aligned deities: Karana, Rodcet Nife, Erollisi Marr, Brell Serilis, Mithaniel Marr, Prexus, Tunare, Quellious. They gain Resurrection at high levels, framed as their deity calling back a fallen champion. The lore bridge for corpse reclamation: the deity who permits resurrection of the soul surely permits reclamation of the vessel; leaving a holy warrior's remains in enemy territory is an affront to their god.

**Shaman (ancestor spirits, totemic tradition)**
Shamans serve as priests to the tribal races (Barbarian, Troll, Ogre, Iksar, Vah Shir). They petition ancestor spirits and totemic forces — the relationship is supplicatory, not commanding. "Tether" (binding/restraining) is necromancer vocabulary; shaman magic calls and guides rather than compels.

**Beastlord (hunter-animal bond, ancestor spirits)**
Introduced in Luclin (Vah Shir and Iksar only at launch — era-legal). Beastlord spiritual vocabulary blends monk discipline, shaman ancestor-appeal, and the primal bond between hunter and beast. "Ancestor's Reclaim" has a grammar flaw (verb-as-noun) and leans exclusively on ancestor-worship, underplaying the beast-bond that defines the class.

**Druid (nature stewardship, Tunare/Karana devotion)**
Druids draw power from nature deities (primarily Tunare and Karana). They are stewards of the living world, not masters of death. A corpse-summon framed as "the natural world recognizes its own and moves the fallen vessel to where it is needed" is consistent; the druid acts as a steward on nature's behalf. "Return to the Soil" implies burial/decomposition — movement away from the caster, not toward them. This directly contradicts the spell's mechanics.

**Ranger (warden/guardian of nature, limited magic)**
Rangers are described as "cousins of the Druid" — nature-affiliated guardians with limited spellcasting. Their magic is nature-assisted rather than deity-invoked. A Ranger's corpse-summon is most naturally framed as nature-assisted wayfinding: the land knows where the fallen are, and the ranger asks it to bring them home. "Hunter's Burden" implies reluctant mournfulness, not purposeful agency.

**Wizard (translocation, arcane physics)**
Wizards are defined by teleportation/translocation as their non-damage specialty. A corpse has coordinates; the wizard calculates and moves it. No deity, no death magic — pure arcane physics. "Spectral" is a slight flavor stretch (ghosts aren't wizard territory) but defensible as "spectral resonance of the body."

**Magician (elemental summoning)**
Magicians summon elemental pets, conjured items, food, water, weapons. A corpse is matter; matter can be summoned. This is the cleanest non-necromancer rationale — no death magic, no deity, pure summoning theory. "Summon Mortal Remains" treats the body as an object of elemental summoning.

**Enchanter (illusion, mind magic)**
Enchanters manipulate perception and mind. The lore stretch: every being leaves a fading impression — an echo of the self. A dead body retains that echo. An Enchanter follows the impression as a psychic thread and draws the physical anchor along with it. The weakest lore fit of all 12 classes, but within the Enchanter's conceptual toolkit of mind-over-matter perception.

**Bard (music as universal weave)**
Bards in EQ use music to produce effects across all magical schools — haste, slow, cold damage, charm, lull. The musical anchor is the constant; the effect school varies. A Bard's corpse-summon: every person carries a resonant echo, the note they struck in life. A skilled Bard plays that note, and the resonance (and thus the body) answers. No necromancy — sympathetic acoustics. "Dirge" is appropriate (funeral lament); "Recovery" deflates the poetic weight.

### Historical Context

The Luclin expansion (era-legal) introduced the Nexus — a planar transit hub bound to Luclin by the Combine Empire and, earlier, the Shissar. This provides an in-world hook for why casters of all traditions have access to a resonance-based retrieval rite: any mortal who has bent the fabric of the world through arcane transit carries a mark that can be read by any sufficiently focused spellcaster.

## Universal Framing Rationales

Two options for the game-designer to present to the user. Both are era-compliant.

### Option A: Compact of the Awakened (recommended)

Following the discovery of Luclin and the Nexus, scholars across Norrath documented an ancient rite predating the Combine — a compact inscribed on the Nexus stones by the same artificers who bound the Shissar. The compact holds that any mortal who has crossed the boundaries of the planes through arcane transit carries a resonant mark on their body. A sufficiently focused spellcaster can use that resonance as a tether, drawing the marked flesh across any mundane distance. The rite was known to necromancers for ages but has now spread to other traditions who found their own interpretations of the underlying principle.

**Why recommended:** Era-grounded in Luclin lore without touching PoP. Explains why all casting classes have access (they've all worked with planar/arcane forces) while non-casters do not. Gives the feature a story hook without requiring new NPC lore.

### Option B: Rite of Mortal Reclamation

The gods of Norrath long ago agreed that a mortal who falls in service to their deity should not be abandoned to the wilds. Each divine or arcane tradition developed its own ritual expression of this principle — clerics through holy invocation, necromancers through death-compact, shamans through ancestor-appeal. The spell exists because the gods allow it.

**Why secondary:** Thematically looser. Works well for divine classes (CLR, PAL) but feels thin for agnostic INT-casters (WIZ, MAG, ENC) who follow no deity. Easier to communicate in a one-sentence description.

## Hard-Fit Class Rationales

**Paladin**
Paladins already have Resurrection — their deity calls back the soul. Extending that to physical remains is a small step: the deity who permits resurrection of the soul permits reclamation of the vessel. Frame the spell as a preparatory rite before resurrection, or as an act of duty: leaving a holy warrior's remains in enemy territory is an affront to their god. Option B framing (Divine Compact) fits Paladins particularly cleanly.

**Ranger**
Rangers are wardens — guardians of territory and those under nature's care. A Ranger would not leave a fallen companion's remains unrecovered any more than they would abandon a wounded ally. The magic is nature-assisted wayfinding: the land knows where the fallen are, and the ranger asks it to bring them home. Avoid all death-magic framing; lean into "nature guides, ranger commands."

**Bard**
Every person carries a resonant echo — the note they struck in life. A skilled Bard plays that note, and the resonance (and thus the body) answers. This is sympathetic acoustics, not necromancy. Bards in EQ produce effects across all magical schools through music; the musical anchor is the constant. "Dirge of Homecoming" frames the song as finding what is lost, not commanding the dead.

**Magician**
A corpse is matter. Matter can be summoned. Magicians summon elemental pets, conjured items, food, water, weapons — the body is simply another object of elemental summoning. No death magic, no deity, no ancestor-appeal. Pure summoning theory. The approved name "Summon Mortal Remains" handles this framing correctly by treating the body as an object, not a ritual focus.

**Enchanter**
Every being leaves a fading impression — an echo of the self. A dead body retains that echo. An Enchanter follows the impression as a psychic thread and draws the physical anchor along with it. This is the weakest lore fit of the five, but it is within the Enchanter's established toolkit of mind-over-matter perception and illusion. "Phantasmal Reclamation" supports this framing; the body is recalled through the phantom of the person it was.
