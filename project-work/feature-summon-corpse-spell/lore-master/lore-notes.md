## Universal Framing Rationale (pick one — or offer the user both)

**Option A: The Compact of the Awakened** (my recommendation)
Following the discovery of Luclin and the Nexus, scholars across Norrath documented an ancient rite predating the Combine — a compact inscribed on the Nexus stones by the same artificers who bound the Shissar to the moon. The compact holds that any mortal who has crossed the boundaries of the planes through arcane transit (i.e., has teleported or otherwise bent the fabric of the world) carries a resonant mark on their body. A sufficiently focused spellcaster can use that resonance as a tether, drawing the marked flesh across any mundane distance. The rite was known to necromancers for ages but has now spread to other traditions who found their own interpretations of the underlying principle. This roots the universality in Luclin-era lore without touching PoP, and explains why all CASTING classes get it (they've all worked with planar forces) while non-casters do not.

**Option B: The Rite of Mortal Reclamation**
An older, simpler rationale: the gods of Norrath long ago agreed that a mortal who falls in service to their deity should not be abandoned to the wilds. Each divine or arcane tradition developed its own ritual expression of this principle — clerics through holy invocation, necromancers through death-compact, shamans through ancestor-appeal. The spell exists because the gods allow it. This is thematically looser but easier to communicate in a one-sentence spell description. Works well for the divine classes (CLR, PAL) but feels thin for INT-casters who follow no deity.

I recommend **Option A** as it is era-grounded (Luclin Nexus lore), explains the INT-caster access without deity gymnastics, and gives the feature a story hook without requiring new NPC lore.

## Spell Name Critique and Final Recommendations

**Necromancer: "Conjure Cadaver"**
Solid. "Conjure" and "cadaver" are both in-register for necromancer vocabulary. Alliterative, clinical, slightly contemptuous of the body. The existing live spell is literally called "Conjure Corpse" at higher tiers, so this fits the progression pattern. APPROVED.

**Shadow Knight: "Death's Recall"**
Good. Shadow Knights use possessive-deity constructions frequently (Innoruuk's Caress, etc.). The possessive "Death's" works, and "recall" implies commanding return — right tone for an unholy warrior. APPROVED.

**Shaman: "Spirit Tether"**
ISSUE — minor. Shamans deal with ancestor spirits and totemic forces, so "Spirit" is correct. But "Tether" implies restraint/binding, which reads more like necromancer territory (binding a spirit). A shaman would more likely "call" or "guide" the spirit home. SUGGEST: **"Ancestral Summons"** or **"Spirit's Beckoning"**. Either fits the tribal, petitionary tone of shaman magic better than a forceful tether.

**Beastlord: "Ancestor's Reclaim"**
ISSUE — grammar ("Ancestor's Reclaim" is awkward; "reclaim" is a verb used as a noun). More critically, Beastlord spiritual vocabulary leans toward the bond between beast and hunter rather than pure ancestor-worship. Vah Shir Beastlords in particular have a cultural identity tied to hunting and the animal spirit. SUGGEST: **"Ancestral Call"** (fixes the grammar, retains the ancestor theme) or **"Feral Reclamation"** (leans into the beast-bond aspect).

**Cleric: "Hallowed Recall"**
ISSUE. "Recall" as a word is fine, but "Hallowed Recall" reads like a Paladin spell — "hallowed" implies sacred blessing of the ground/object itself. For a Cleric, whose power flows from deity authority rather than personal holiness, a more invocatory name fits better. SUGGEST: **"Divine Reclamation"** or **"Blessed Retrieval"**. Both convey a deity granting the return of the fallen servant's remains.

**Paladin: "Solemn Retrieval"**
APPROVED — but I have a preferred alternate. "Solemn" captures the gravity of death and holy duty. However, Paladins are active agents of their deity's will, and "retrieval" sounds like an errand rather than a sacred act. SUGGEST: **"Sacred Vigil"** or keep "Solemn Retrieval" — it works. Not a blocking issue.

**Druid: "Return to the Soil"**
ISSUE — significant. The corpse comes TO the caster, not to the earth. "Return to the Soil" implies burial, decomposition, a body being surrendered to the cycle of nature. That is exactly the opposite of what this spell does. This is the most semantically broken name in the table. SUGGEST: **"Nature's Reclamation"** or **"Cycle's Claim"**. The framing should be that the natural world recognizes the body and moves it to where it is needed — the caster is a steward acting on nature's behalf.

**Ranger: "Hunter's Burden"**
ISSUE — tone. "Burden" implies the ranger is dragging the corpse back reluctantly, which conflicts with the spell's function (summoning the corpse to you). More critically, Rangers are NOT dark or mournful — they are protectors and trackers. "Burden" is self-flagellating in a way that doesn't fit Ranger flavor. SUGGEST: **"Warden's Claim"** — a ranger is a guardian/warden; claiming a fallen companion's remains to ensure proper rites fits. Alternatively **"Trail's End"** for a more poetic, nature-flavored option.

**Wizard: "Spectral Translocation"**
APPROVED with note. Wizards are defined by teleportation and translocation as their non-damage specialty. "Spectral" is a slight stretch (ghosts aren't wizard territory), but the word functions here as "of or relating to the spectrum / resonance of the body" which is defensible for an INT-caster rationalizing corpse-physics. If the game-designer wants a cleaner option: **"Arcane Retrieval"** works. Not a blocking issue — keeping "Spectral Translocation" is fine.

**Magician: "Summon Mortal Remains"**
APPROVED. Magicians are defined as summoners — summoning items, pets, elemental forces. "Summon Mortal Remains" is exactly on-brand. Clinical, direct, magician-appropriate. The phrase "mortal remains" is also common enough to feel natural. Strong name.

**Enchanter: "Phantasmal Reclamation"**
APPROVED with note. Enchanters work with illusion and mind magic, and "phantasmal" fits their school well enough — the idea being they reach the impression/echo of the body and use it as a handle to draw the physical remains. Slight stretch, but defensible. Alternative if preferred: **"Illusory Tether"** — more evocative of how an enchanter would conceptualize the effect.

**Bard: "Dirge of Recovery"**
ISSUE — minor. A dirge is a funeral lament — appropriate for the theme, and Bards dealing in death-adjacent music is established (they have lullabies that put things to sleep, etc.). However "Recovery" deflates the poetic weight of "Dirge." SUGGEST: **"Dirge of Homecoming"** or **"Lament of Reclamation"** — keeps the musical funeral framing while giving it more evocative weight.

## Hard-Fit Classes — Thematic Rationales

**Paladin**
Paladins already have Resurrection — they call back the soul by their deity's authority. Extending that to the physical remains is a small step: the deity who permits resurrection of the soul surely permits reclamation of the vessel. Frame the spell as a preparatory rite — the paladin calls the body to them so that proper last rites can be performed before resurrection, or simply because leaving a holy warrior's remains in enemy territory is an affront to their god. Deity-grants-mercy framing (Option B above) works cleanly here.

**Ranger**
Rangers are defined by their mastery of territory and their role as wardens. In Classic EQ lore, Rangers are explicitly tied to the protection of nature and those under its care. A Ranger retrieving a fallen comrade's remains is consistent with their warden role — they would not leave a body unrecovered any more than they would leave a fallen companion on the field. The magic is nature-assisted wayfinding: the forest knows where the body is, and the ranger asks it to bring the body home. Avoid death-magic framing entirely; lean into "nature guides, ranger commands."

**Bard**
Bards in EQ use music to weave magical effects across every school — they have songs that duplicate haste, slow, cold damage, charming, and lulling. The musical anchor is the constant; the effect school varies by song. A Bard's corpse-summons works because every person carries a resonant echo — the note they struck in life — and a skilled enough Bard can play that note and call the resonance (and thus the body) back. This is pure Bardic lore: music as the thread that connects all things. No necromancy involved; it is sympathetic acoustics. "Dirge of Homecoming" reinforces this — the song finds what is lost.

**Magician**
Magicians summon everything: elemental pets, water, food, weapon procs, rings. The body is simply another object to summon. Their framing is the most mechanically clean of the hard-fit classes — they have no lore conflict, only flavor awkwardness (summoning a corpse rather than a conjured weapon). "Summon Mortal Remains" solves this by treating the body as an object of summoning, not a death ritual. The rationale is: a corpse is matter; matter can be summoned. No deity, no ancestor-magic, pure elemental summoning theory. The approved name handles this correctly.

**Enchanter**
Enchanters work with illusion, charm, and the mind. The lore stretch is real — they do not manipulate matter or death. The rationale that works: Enchanters perceive every being as an impression or pattern they can reach with their mind. A dead body still retains the fading impression of the person it was. An Enchanter who knew that person can find and follow that impression like a psychic thread, and by pulling the thread, move the physical anchor attached to it. It is the least clean fit but entirely within Enchanter's conceptual toolkit of mind-over-matter perception. "Phantasmal Reclamation" supports this framing.

## Issues Requiring Resolution Before PRD Finalization

BLOCKING:
1. Druid "Return to the Soil" — name contradicts the spell's direction. Must be changed.
2. Shaman "Spirit Tether" — tone mismatch (binding vs. petitioning). Recommend "Ancestral Summons."
3. Ranger "Hunter's Burden" — tone mismatch (grief vs. agency). Recommend "Warden's Claim."
4. Bard "Dirge of Recovery" — deflated. Recommend "Dirge of Homecoming."
5. Beastlord "Ancestor's Reclaim" — grammar issue and slight tone mismatch. Recommend "Ancestral Call."
6. Cleric "Hallowed Recall" — reads as Paladin, not Cleric. Recommend "Divine Reclamation."

NON-BLOCKING (can keep originals if preferred):
7. Paladin "Solemn Retrieval" — works, but "Sacred Vigil" is more Paladin-coded.
8. Wizard "Spectral Translocation" — slight flavor stretch but defensible.
9. Enchanter "Phantasmal Reclamation" — approved as-is.
